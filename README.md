# CORELINE Smart Spine Corset

## Adaptive spinal support for work, movement, and everyday life.

CORELINE is an open-development project focused on creating a next-generation spinal support system combining biomechanics, advanced materials, modular mechanical design, and future smart integration.

The project is currently focused on developing and evaluating the mechanical CORELINE prototype. Sensor systems and companion software are planned as optional future development layers.

---

## Mission

Our mission is to develop an adaptive spinal support system designed to explore improved load distribution, posture support, and comfortable movement — built transparently and collaboratively.

CORELINE is being developed as an engineering and wearable-design project. Current prototypes are intended for technical evaluation and development and should not be interpreted as medical devices or validated medical treatments.

---

## What Is CORELINE?

CORELINE Smart Corset is being developed as a lightweight, breathable, and modular spinal support system.

The mechanical concept is intended to explore:

- lumbar and thoracic support;
- load distribution across the wearable structure;
- adjustable levels of mechanical support;
- comfortable movement during everyday activity;
- modular construction and replaceable components;
- integration of different structural materials;
- future integration of pressure and motion sensors.

The system is designed to work mechanically first.

Sensors, electronics, mobile applications, and data analysis are considered optional future development layers rather than dependencies of the core mechanical concept.

## Development Approach

CORELINE is being developed iteratively, with an emphasis on practical mechanical support first.

Current development priorities include:

- mechanical design and fit;
- wearability and comfort;
- material selection;
- prototype construction;
- structural evaluation;
- adjustment range;
- modular construction;
- documentation;
- prototype testing and validation;
- future integration of sensors and software.

The development sequence is intentionally mechanical-first.

The initial goal is to understand how the physical structure behaves before introducing additional complexity through electronics and software.

Smart features are therefore considered a future development layer.

The core mechanical concept does not depend on electronics to function.

## Future Sensor Integration

A future CORELINE sensor edition may introduce electronic measurement capabilities.

Potential sensor functions include:

- pressure measurement;
- pressure-distribution monitoring;
- inertial measurement using an IMU;
- orientation and movement measurements;
- battery monitoring;
- Bluetooth Low Energy communication.

The sensor architecture is currently provisional.

Electronics, sensor placement, communication protocols, calibration procedures, and data formats may change during development.

The mechanical CORELINE system is intended to remain functional independently of the sensor module.

---

## Data and Privacy Direction

Future sensor and software development is intended to follow a privacy-first approach.

Design goals include:

- local storage by default;
- minimal collection of personally identifiable information;
- explicit user control over data sharing;
- explicit user control over data export;
- no mandatory cloud account for basic operation;
- clear data deletion and retention controls;
- encrypted communication where technically supported.

Future implementations should not transmit sensor data to external services without explicit user action or consent.

The final privacy architecture will depend on the hardware and software implementations selected during development.

## Development Principles

CORELINE development follows several basic principles:

**Mechanical first**

The wearable should provide its core mechanical function without requiring electronics or software.

**Modular development**

Mechanical components, sensors, electronics, and software should be developed as separable layers where practical.

**Prototype before production**

Early designs are experimental and are expected to change based on testing.

**Document uncertainty**

Provisional specifications should be identified as provisional rather than presented as finalized engineering decisions.

**Privacy by design**

Future connected features should minimize unnecessary collection and transmission of user data.

**Open development**

Relevant technical progress, design decisions, and prototype results should be documented openly where practical.
