# Research References

## CORELINE Scientific and Engineering Background

This document collects selected scientific literature relevant to the
biomechanical principles being investigated during development of the
CORELINE Smart Spine Corset.

The references below inform design hypotheses, prototype development,
testing methods, and future sensor integration.

> **Important:** These publications provide background scientific and
> engineering evidence. They do not constitute clinical validation of
> CORELINE itself. Product-specific performance claims will require
> dedicated testing and validation.

---

## 1. Lumbar Spine Loading and Posture

### Takahashi et al. — Mechanical Load of the Lumbar Spine

Takahashi I., Kikuchi S., Sato K., Sato N.  
**Mechanical load of the lumbar spine during forward bending motion of
the trunk — a biomechanical study.**  
*Spine.* 2006;31(1):18–23.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1097/01.brs.0000192636.69129.fb

**Relevance to CORELINE**

This study investigates how trunk posture and external loading affect
mechanical loads acting on the lumbar spine.

Relevant design areas:

- spinal load redistribution;
- posture-dependent loading;
- biomechanical validation;
- support during forward trunk movement.

---

### Khoddam-Khorasani, Arjmand & Shirazi-Adl — Lumbar Posture and Spinal Loads

Khoddam-Khorasani P., Arjmand N., Shirazi-Adl A.  
**Effect of changes in the lumbar posture in lifting on trunk muscle and
spinal loads: A combined in vivo, musculoskeletal, and finite element
model study.**  
*Journal of Biomechanics.* 2020;104:109728.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2020.109728

**Relevance to CORELINE**

The study demonstrates that lumbar posture influences muscle activity,
compression, shear forces, and load sharing within spinal structures.

Relevant design areas:

- adaptive spinal support;
- controlled movement rather than rigid immobilization;
- posture-dependent mechanical behavior;
- biomechanical simulation.

## 6. CORELINE Research Direction

The literature above supports several engineering questions currently
being investigated during CORELINE development:

1. How can external support redistribute load without unnecessarily
   restricting natural movement?
2. How should support stiffness vary across lumbar and thoracic regions?
3. How can interface pressure be distributed to improve comfort during
   prolonged wear?
4. How can adjustable compression accommodate different body types and
   activities?
5. Can embedded pressure and motion sensors provide useful information
   about posture, movement, and support distribution?

These questions will be evaluated through iterative prototyping,
mechanical testing, wear testing, and future sensor-assisted
experiments.
