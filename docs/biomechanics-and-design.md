# Biomechanics and Design

CORELINE is being developed around general principles of spinal biomechanics, mobility, load distribution, and wearable support.

The concepts described here represent the current design approach and are subject to further testing and validation.

---

## Biomechanical Foundation

The human spine is a dynamic structure that combines:

- mobility
- load distribution
- muscular engagement
- postural control

CORELINE is being designed to provide external support without intentionally immobilizing normal movement.

The current design goals are to:

- assist postural support
- distribute mechanical support across selected regions
- preserve practical freedom of movement
- provide a wearable platform suitable for further testing

These goals are design objectives and should not be interpreted as validated clinical outcomes.

---

## Load Distribution Concept

The CORELINE concept uses distributed support across lumbar and thoracic regions rather than relying on a single rigid support point.

The design is being explored with the aim of:

- distributing support across multiple contact areas
- avoiding excessive localized pressure
- supporting comfortable positioning
- maintaining mobility during everyday use

The effectiveness of these concepts will be evaluated through prototype testing and further validation.

---

## Design Constraints

The current design approach prioritizes:

- No forced immobilization
- No intentionally excessive compression
- Adjustable and modular support
- Practical freedom of movement
- Wearability during everyday activities

The system is intended to support natural biomechanics rather than replace normal muscular function.

---

## Development Status

The biomechanical concepts described in this document are part of an active prototype and development process.

CORELINE is not presented here as a medically validated device, and no therapeutic or clinical outcome is claimed at this stage.

Design specifications may change as prototypes are tested and evaluated.
