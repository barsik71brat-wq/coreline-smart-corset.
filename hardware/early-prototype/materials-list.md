# Early Prototype Materials

## CORELINE Mechanical Prototype

This document describes material categories used or considered during early
CORELINE mechanical prototyping.

It is **not a production bill of materials (BOM)** and should not be interpreted
as a final manufacturing specification.

> **Development status:** Early prototype / material evaluation  
> Materials, constructions, suppliers, thicknesses, grades, and manufacturing
> processes remain subject to change as testing progresses.

---

## Purpose

Early prototypes are intended to evaluate:

- overall geometry;
- support distribution;
- flexibility;
- adjustment range;
- comfort;
- breathability;
- structural behavior; and
- manufacturability.

Material selection at this stage is therefore experimental.

---

## Prototype Material Architecture

A typical prototype may contain several functional material groups:

| Functional group | Purpose | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| Skin-contact textile | Comfort and moisture management | Under evaluation |
| Structural textile | Load transfer and dimensional stability | Under evaluation |
| Padding / spacer layer | Local cushioning and pressure distribution | Under evaluation |
| Flexible support elements | Controlled structural reinforcement | Under evaluation |
| Elastic elements | Dynamic fit and movement accommodation | Under evaluation |
| Adjustment straps | User-controlled tension | Under evaluation |
| Fasteners | Reversible adjustment and assembly | Under evaluation |
| Edge finishing | Comfort and durability | Under evaluation |

No material listed here should be considered production-approved.

---

## 1. Skin-Contact Layer

The inner layer should provide a comfortable interface between the wearable
structure and the user.

Candidate constructions may include:

- breathable knit textiles;
- spacer fabrics;
- moisture-managing synthetic fabrics;
- soft mesh structures; and
- other wearable-grade textile constructions.

Evaluation criteria include:

- surface comfort;
- breathability;
- moisture behavior;
- stretch;
- recovery;
- abrasion resistance;
- seam compatibility; and
- repeated-wear performance.

Final fiber composition has not been selected.

---

## 2. Structural Textile Layer

A structural textile layer may be used to transfer loads between adjustment
elements and support regions.

Candidate materials may include:

- low-stretch woven textiles;
- reinforced mesh;
- technical knit structures;
- laminated textile assemblies; and
- locally reinforced fabric constructions.

The required stiffness should be established through prototype testing.

Excessive stiffness may reduce comfort and mobility, while insufficient
stiffness may reduce the effectiveness of load transfer.

---

## 3. Padding and Spacer Materials

Soft intermediate layers may be used in selected contact regions.

Potential materials include:

- spacer mesh;
- open-cell foam;
- flexible polymer foam;
- textile padding; and
- multilayer textile structures.

The term **"smart foam"** should not be used unless a specific material and
measurable functional behavior have been defined.

Padding should be evaluated for:

- compression behavior;
- recovery;
- heat retention;
- moisture retention;
- durability;
- thickness stability; and
- local pressure distribution.

---

## 4. Flexible Structural Supports

Early prototypes may use flexible reinforcement elements to investigate
structural support.

Candidate technologies may include:

- polymer stays;
- spring elements;
- composite strips;
- textile-reinforced structures; and
- other flexible reinforcement concepts.

Carbon-fiber components may be investigated, but carbon fiber is **not
currently defined as a required production material**.

Structural elements should be evaluated for:

- bending stiffness;
- torsional behavior;
- fatigue resistance;
- edge safety;
- mass;
- manufacturability;
- attachment method; and
- user comfort.

---

## 5. Elastic Components

Elastic elements may be used where controlled extension is required.

Candidate components include:

- elastic webbing;
- stretch textile panels;
- elastic tapes; and
- engineered knit zones.

Relevant properties include:

- force-extension behavior;
- recovery;
- hysteresis;
- fatigue;
- width stability; and
- resistance to repeated cycling.

Elastic components should be characterized mechanically before final
specification.

---

## 6. Adjustment Straps

Adjustment straps are used to modify fit and local tension.

Potential constructions include:

- non-elastic webbing;
- elastic webbing;
- multilayer textile straps; and
- hybrid elastic/non-elastic assemblies.

The design should allow adjustment without producing excessive local pressure
or restricting normal movement.

---

## 7. Fasteners

Prototype fastening systems may include:

- hook-and-loop fasteners;
- buckles;
- ladder locks;
- low-profile hardware; and
- other reversible fastening systems.

> "Velcro" is a trademark. Generic engineering documentation should normally
> use the term **hook-and-loop fastener** unless referring specifically to a
> product supplied under the VELCRO® brand.

Fasteners should be evaluated for:

- holding force;
- ease of adjustment;
- accidental release;
- cycle life;
- textile compatibility; and
- comfort.

---

## 8. Edge Finishing and Binding

Edges that may contact the user should be finished to minimize irritation.

Candidate approaches include:

- elastic binding;
- folded textile edges;
- soft woven tape;
- bonded edges; and
- covered seam constructions.

Edge construction should be evaluated during extended wear testing.

---

## Material Selection Criteria

Prototype materials should be compared using documented criteria rather than
appearance alone.

Suggested criteria include:

| Property | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Mass | Overall wearable comfort |
| Thickness | Bulk and fit |
| Bending stiffness | Structural behavior |
| Tensile behavior | Load transfer |
| Stretch and recovery | Dynamic fit |
| Compression response | Local comfort |
| Breathability | Thermal comfort |
| Moisture behavior | Long-duration wear |
| Abrasion resistance | Durability |
| Fatigue resistance | Repeated use |
| Wash compatibility | Maintenance |
| Skin-contact suitability | User safety |
| Manufacturability | Production feasibility |
| Availability | Supply-chain feasibility |
| Cost | Future scalability |
