# Early Prototype Dimensions and Fit

## CORELINE Mechanical Prototype

This document describes the dimensional and fitting approach used during early development of the CORELINE mechanical prototype.

The dimensions described here are **development parameters, not final production specifications**. Geometry, sizing, adjustment ranges, panel dimensions, and component placement may change as prototype testing progresses.

> **Development status:** Early prototype / ergonomic validation  
> Final dimensions and size ranges have not yet been established.

---

## Purpose

The dimensional design of the early prototype is intended to evaluate:

- overall torso coverage;
- ergonomic fit;
- freedom of movement;
- load distribution;
- positioning of structural support elements;
- compression and adjustment behavior;
- interaction between textile and semi-rigid components;
- comfort during sitting, standing, and movement; and
- suitability of the architecture for multiple body types.

At this stage, the objective is not to define a finished commercial sizing system, but to establish a repeatable framework for prototype development and testing.

---

## Design Reference System

Prototype geometry is considered relative to anatomical regions rather than fixed universal dimensions.

Primary reference regions include:

- upper thoracic region;
- lower thoracic region;
- lumbar region;
- waist;
- lateral torso;
- abdominal region; and
- shoulder and upper-back transition areas where applicable.

Structural elements should follow the intended support zones while avoiding unnecessary restriction of natural torso movement.

Exact anatomical placement remains subject to ergonomic testing.

---

## Prototype Geometry

The mechanical prototype uses a body-contoured architecture consisting of several interacting zones.

### Posterior support zone

The posterior section provides the primary structural interface along the back.

Its geometry is intended to:

- follow the general contour of the thoracic and lumbar regions;
- distribute mechanical support over a broad area;
- provide mounting locations for flexible or semi-rigid support elements;
- reduce isolated pressure points; and
- preserve controlled spinal and torso movement.

The final length, width, curvature, and segmentation of this section remain under evaluation.

### Lateral transition zones

The lateral sections connect the posterior structure with the anterior adjustment system.

These areas are designed to:

- accommodate torso expansion and movement;
- transfer tension between front and rear sections;
- maintain stable positioning during normal activity; and
- minimize excessive material accumulation around the waist.

### Anterior adjustment zone

The front section provides adjustable closure and compression.

The prototype should allow sufficient adjustment to evaluate different:

- torso circumferences;
- compression levels;
- closure positions; and
- tension distributions.

The closure architecture may change between prototype iterations.

## Sizing Strategy

The early prototype does **not** yet use a finalized commercial size chart.

Prototype fitting is based on a combination of:

- torso circumference;
- waist circumference;
- torso length;
- relative thoracic-to-waist geometry;
- required adjustment range; and
- observed fit during movement.

Future production versions may use standardized size categories after sufficient anthropometric and wear-test data have been collected.

Potential production sizing may eventually include multiple sizes rather than relying on a single highly adjustable configuration.

No final sizing structure has been approved.

---

## Adjustment Range

Adjustment mechanisms are an important part of the prototype architecture.

They are intended to allow:

- controlled circumferential compression;
- adaptation to differences in torso geometry;
- compensation for clothing layers;
- tuning of support intensity; and
- repeatable positioning of the prototype.

Adjustment range should be sufficient for experimental fitting without creating excessive strap length, material overlap, or localized pressure.

The required production adjustment range will be determined through testing.

---

## Structural Element Placement

Flexible or semi-rigid structural elements should be positioned according to functional support zones rather than arbitrary fixed spacing.

Prototype evaluation should consider:

- symmetry;
- distance from the spinal centerline;
- interaction with the lumbar region;
- curvature during torso flexion and extension;
- lateral bending;
- sitting posture;
- edge pressure; and
- migration of components during movement.

Structural components should not create sharp transitions or concentrated pressure against the body.

Exact placement remains experimental.

---

## Fit Principles

A correctly fitted prototype should:

- remain stable during normal movement;
- distribute tension across multiple areas;
- avoid obvious concentrated pressure points;
- permit normal breathing;
- permit functional torso movement;
- avoid excessive compression;
- avoid uncontrolled migration during use; and
- allow adjustment without requiring permanent modification.

The prototype should support the intended mechanical architecture without functioning as a rigid immobilization device.

---

## Prototype Fitting Procedure

A typical development fitting sequence is:

1. Place the prototype around the torso in a neutral standing position.
2. Align the posterior section with the intended back support region.
3. Confirm that the lower and upper boundaries are positioned as intended.
4. Close the primary anterior adjustment system without excessive tension.
5. Adjust secondary straps or tension elements gradually and symmetrically.
6. Check the position of structural elements.
7. Verify that normal breathing is unrestricted.
8. Evaluate fit while standing.
9. Evaluate fit while sitting.
10. Perform basic torso flexion, extension, rotation, and lateral movement.
11. Inspect for component migration, edge pressure, folding, or excessive compression.
12. Record observations before changing the prototype geometry.

Adjustment should be progressive rather than immediately applying maximum available tension.

---

## Fit Evaluation

Each prototype iteration should be evaluated for:

### Static fit

Assessment while:

- standing;
- sitting; and
- maintaining a neutral posture.

### Dynamic fit

Assessment during controlled:

- walking;
- sitting and standing transitions;
- torso rotation;
- forward bending; and
- lateral movement.

### Comfort

Evaluation should consider:

- pressure distribution;
- edge contact;
- heat accumulation;
- textile bunching;
- strap interaction;
- skin-interface comfort; and
- perceived restriction of movement.

Observations should be documented for comparison between prototype iterations.

## Dimensional Change Control

When prototype geometry is modified, the revision should document:

- which dimension changed;
- previous configuration;
- new configuration;
- reason for the change;
- expected effect;
- observed result; and
- prototype revision identifier.

Changing several major geometric variables simultaneously should be avoided where practical because it makes comparison between iterations more difficult.

---

## Manufacturing Considerations

Dimensions that perform well during hand-built prototyping may require modification for manufacturing.

Future dimensional development should consider:

- seam allowances;
- textile stretch;
- cutting tolerances;
- reinforcement geometry;
- closure attachment areas;
- component thickness;
- assembly tolerances;
- repeatability between units; and
- manufacturing method.

Therefore, prototype dimensions should not automatically be transferred into production drawings without design-for-manufacturing review.

---

## Safety and Development Notes

The early prototype is an experimental mechanical support system.

During development:

- excessive compression should be avoided;
- sharp or exposed structural edges should not contact the body;
- damaged structural components should be replaced;
- fit should be checked after significant geometry changes;
- movement and breathing should not be unnecessarily restricted; and
- prototype observations should be documented before further iteration.

This document does not provide clinical fitting instructions.
